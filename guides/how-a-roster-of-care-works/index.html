Guide

How a roster of care works

A roster of care sets out the support in a shared home across an ordinary week. Here is what it shows, who writes it, how it relates to your funding and what to check.

Reading speed

Uses your device's voice. If you use a screen reader you may not need this.

Key points

  • A roster of care describes the support you get at home in an ordinary week and how your provider will deliver it.
  • Your provider writes it with you or your nominee, and you can ask for a copy at any time.
  • The NDIS says a roster of care is not a requirement and does not decide the amount or type of support in your plan.
  • The NDIA decides home and living funding from evidence of your support needs, usually from your treating health professionals.
  • You can make minor changes with your provider if they fit within the funding in your plan.
  • The roster is not your agreement, so check your service agreement matches what the roster shows.

What a roster of care is

A roster of care is a picture of the support in a home across an ordinary week. People use it in supported independent living (the NDIS calls it SIL).

SIL is NDIS funding for a support worker to help or supervise you at home, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. It is usually shared between housemates who each have NDIS funding.

The NDIS describes a roster of care as a tool. It breaks down your weekly support needs and how the supports will be delivered. It helps you and your provider know what support is in the home each week.

Is a roster of care still used

Yes, but it is optional. The NDIS guide for SIL providers says a roster of care “isn’t a requirement”. It also says providers do not need to submit one to the NDIA.

The NDIA still publishes a roster of care tool for providers. It is a spreadsheet with a written guide. The current guide is version 4, dated June 2026. It says again that completing the tool is not a requirement.

So the roster has not been retired. Its job is to help you and your provider plan and agree. It is not a form that sets your funding.

Who writes it

Your SIL provider writes the roster. They should not write it alone.

The NDIA’s guide says providers must consult the participant, or their nominee, to put the information together. The NDIS also says you and your provider should agree on the roster and on how your plan funding will be claimed.

You can ask your provider for your roster of care at any time.

What a roster of care shows

The NDIS guideline on SIL says your roster of care includes:

  • the hours of support you need for day-to-day activities at home in an ordinary week
  • the ratio of support you need
  • the level of support you need, for example standard or high intensity
  • the type of support you need overnight
  • the amount of irregular support you need.

Here is what those words mean.

Ratio of support

The ratio is how many workers support how many people at one time. The NDIS gives the example of one support worker supporting 2 participants in a home.

The NDIA’s tool asks the provider to list every person who lives in the home. That includes housemates who are not NDIS participants, without naming them. The tool needs this to show the staffing in the home.

Standard or high intensity

There are 2 sets of hourly prices for supports including SIL: standard and high intensity. The NDIS says most people will need standard support.

Overnight support

The NDIA’s guide describes two kinds of overnight shift:

  • Active overnight. The worker needs to be awake during normal sleeping hours to support you.
  • Sleepover. The worker can sleep, and gets up to help if you need it.

Irregular support

Irregular support covers days that do not go to plan. One example is being unwell and staying home when you would usually be out.

The NDIS says it generally funds 10 irregular support days a year for standard support needs. For high intensity support needs it generally funds 15.

Time away from home

The roster only covers SIL. The NDIS says only one support can be claimed for each period of time. Time out in the community is covered by the community supports in your plan, not by SIL.

How it relates to your SIL funding

The roster of care does not set your funding. The NDIS guideline says a roster of care “doesn’t decide the amount or type of NDIS supports you will get in your plan”. It only describes the supports you can get from your provider.

Your SIL funding depends on your disability support needs. The NDIA’s guide says it may use a roster of care, with other supporting information, when it works out your supports. The roster is one piece of information. The NDIA makes the decision.

The same guide says the roster is not the exact schedule of supports you will get. It is also not an agreement with the NDIA. The actual supports must be agreed with you, or your nominee, once the NDIA approves your plan funding.

SIL funding cannot be used for rent, groceries or other day-to-day expenses. A roster of care should not include them.

How it relates to asking for home and living supports

A roster of care is not how you ask the NDIA for SIL. The NDIS sets out three steps:

  1. Think about what you may need. Make a list of your support needs, your goals and the help you already get.
  2. Talk to your my NDIS contact about your home and living options.
  3. Give the NDIA your evidence.

The evidence should be recent. It should come from a treating health professional, such as an occupational therapist. It should explain your daily support and housing needs, including how often and when you need support each day.

If the NDIA includes home and living supports in your plan, it invites you to a plan meeting. If it does not, it tells you why. You can ask for a review of the decision.

Read how to ask for home and living supports on the NDIS website.

What you and your family should check

Ask for the roster in a format you can use. Then go through it with someone you choose.

  • Does the week look like your week? Check wake-up times, meals, work, appointments and time with family and friends.
  • Is the ratio clear? Ask when you share a worker and when you have a worker to yourself.
  • Is overnight support right? Check whether nights are active or sleepover, and whether that matches what you need.
  • Is the level right? Check whether the roster says standard or high intensity, and ask why.
  • Are irregular days included? Ask how sick days and other unplanned days at home are covered.
  • Does it stay inside SIL? Rent, food and bills should not appear in it.
  • Does it match your service agreement? The NDIS says a SIL service agreement should cover the ratio, amount and intensity of your supports.
  • What happens if a housemate moves out? The NDIS does not fund vacancies. It says your provider should not raise your costs or claim extra from your plan to cover one.

Changing your roster of care

SIL is flexible. The NDIS says you can make minor changes to your roster of care that fit within your funding. Talk about them with your provider. You do not need to tell the NDIA about minor changes that still fit your funding.

A big change is different. If your situation or support needs change a lot, you can ask the NDIA to reassess your plan. The NDIA will want current information about your needs.

If something does not look right

Start with your provider. Ask them to explain the roster in plain words and to fix anything that is wrong. Your support coordinator, if you have one, can help.

If you want to talk through how support is arranged in a shared home, read about supported independent living with Feelable.
