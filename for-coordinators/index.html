The intake pathway

Five steps from referral to the first shift. We do not print timeframes we have not confirmed. Ask intake for a straight answer on your referral.

The five steps, from referral to first shift

  1. You send a referral

    Use the referral form, call or email intake. Give initials only. Tell us the support, the area and the timing.

  2. We tell you if we can help

    You get a plain yes, no or not yet for that support in that area. If it is a no, we say so and do not hold the referral.

  3. We meet the person

    We meet the participant and whoever they want with them. We ask what they want, how their day runs and what worries them. For a home, they can visit before deciding.

  4. We agree it in writing

    The participant gets a service agreement that sets out the supports, the cost and how to end it. A tenancy, where there is one, is a separate document.

  5. Support starts and we report back

    We agree with the participant what we report to you and how often. Then support starts, and you hear from us on that schedule.

Documents we need, and when

We ask for the least we can at each point. We confirm the exact list in writing for each referral.

At first contact, please do not send a diagnosis, an NDIS number, a plan, a date of birth, or any health or behaviour report. We do not need them to tell you whether we can help.

At first contact
  • Participant initials, not the full name. On the referral form, support coordinators and hospital staff give them in part 3.
  • The support and the area
  • Timing, and how the plan is managed
  • Your name, organisation and how to reach you
What we ask for later, with the participant's consent
Once we have said we can help, with the participant's consent
  • The parts of the NDIS plan that cover the supports we will deliver
  • Current support plans and assessments that our workers must follow
  • For supported independent living: the home and living decision and any roster of care
  • For a person leaving hospital: the items on our hospital to home page
Before support starts
  • A signed service agreement
  • Consent for who we may share information with
  • Emergency contacts and current medication information

Plain answers to the questions coordinators ask

How does a person start?

With a referral from you, from the participant or from their family. It costs nothing to ask. Nothing is agreed until the participant has a written service agreement and has had time to read it.

How is SIL different from SDA?

SDA (specialist disability accommodation) is the home. SIL (supported independent living) is the support in it. They are funded separately and have separate agreements.

A tenancy must not depend on taking support from a particular provider. Where someone lives and who supports them are separate choices.

SIL, SDA, ILO and MTA explained

What does the NDIS pay, and what does the participant pay?

SIL funding pays for support workers. It does not pay for rent, groceries or other day-to-day expenses. SDA funding, where a person has it, pays the SDA provider.

Our prices follow the NDIS Pricing Schedule. The participant gets the cost in writing before anyone signs.

What SIL funding covers and what you pay for yourself

What about rent and board?

The participant pays rent and bills from their own money. We do not print amounts here because they differ from home to home. We will confirm rent and board in writing for a home before a person decides.

Who pays for meals?

The NDIS does not pay for food. Support workers can help plan, shop and cook where that is in the person's plan. We will confirm in writing how food costs are shared in each home.

Can a person keep their own support worker?

Participants keep their own support coordinator, plan manager and therapists. They can also keep using another provider for supports outside the home.

In a shared home the roster is shared between housemates. Whether a worker the person already knows can join it depends on the home. Ask us, and we will confirm in writing.

Do you offer individualised living options?

No. We do not offer individualised living options (ILO) as a service. Our guide explains how ILO differs from SIL, so a participant can weigh it up.

How ILO compares with SIL

How do transfers and transition plans work?

Changing provider is the participant's choice, in either direction. We work with the current provider and with you on a written handover, so support does not stop in between. Notice periods are set out in the service agreement. We will confirm the plan for each move in writing.

How to change SIL providers

How is quality assured?

We are a registered NDIS provider, so we must follow the NDIS Code of Conduct and the NDIS Practice Standards, and we are subject to audits. Our audit against the new SIL standards has not happened yet.

How we keep people safe

How do we give feedback?

Tell the person you deal with, or use the feedback and complaints form, which can be anonymous. Anyone can also complain to the NDIS Commission on 1800 035 544.

Allied health and behaviour practitioners

We do not provide therapy or behaviour support. We carry out the plans you write. With the participant's consent, send us the plan and show our workers how you want it done.

We follow it as written and tell you what we see. If part of a plan is not working, we tell you. We do not change it ourselves.

To send a plan or arrange a time to show our workers, email intake@feelable.com.au or call 03 5550 0142.

Housing and SDA providers

If you own or manage a home and want a registered provider for supported independent living in it, talk to us. The tenancy and the support stay separate, with separate agreements, and the tenant chooses who supports them.

We are also registered for specialist disability accommodation.

To talk about a home, email intake@feelable.com.au or call 03 5550 0142.

Conflict of interest

We offer Level 1 and Level 2 support coordination. We also deliver other supports. When one provider does both for the same person, that is a conflict of interest. The NDIA says it must be avoided, or declared and managed.

How we manage it
  • We tell the participant about the conflict in writing before they agree to anything.
  • The participant chooses their providers. We never make one of our supports a condition of another.
  • A participant who uses our other supports can keep their own support coordinator.
  • The participant can change coordinator or provider at any time.

We will confirm in writing how coordination is kept separate from our other supports for each participant. Support coordination at Feelable
